Semantics is a subfield of linguistics and philosophy that studies meaning in language &mdash; in written and other forms. The _semantics of an expression_ such as a symbol, word, language fragment, gesture, etc. are what the expression actually _means_ and what _effects_ it causes in given contexts. Different contexts, languages, cultures and even people assign different meanings to one and the same expression. Words have multiple differing meanings; «bank», «date» or «sanction»<sup>1, 2, 3</sup> are the usual suspects, but beware of the heinous «customer», «account», «product», etc.<sup>4</sup>

Confusion and misunderstandings between people mostly root in vague or differing semantics, or in both. People have different understandings of the seemingly same «thing». Such discrepancies can take months to uncover. They are a cause for much waste and frustration, and they can seriously hamper projects.{.more-air}

This is why transparent, precise and aligned semantics merit our full attention.{.take-away}

## The Semantic Eye

The initiative is named Semantic Eye for its defining _curious mindset_ and its _focus on precision and correctness_, for which proper and aligned semantics are an important component.

A semantics-aware mind develops a «semantic eye» that{.p-left-aligned .more-air}

- looks and obvserves carefully;
- discerns true meanings (_semantics_) of things;
- _understands_ actual _causes and effects_;
- closely examines context and goals before jumping to conclusions or premature action.

## About the Logo

In case you're wondering why the logo of the Semantic Eye _is not an eye_, then be warned that it _is not a hand either_. The pun harkens back to the famous 1929 painting by René Magritte called _La trahison des images_ (The treachery of images) showing a realistically-painted smoking pipe and the words:

<figure>
    <img src="/assets/about/magritte-trahison des images.jpg" alt="La trahison des images" width="378">

    <figcaption>La trahison des images («This is not a pipe»), Source: [[4]](/pages/references/index.html#4)</figcaption>
</figure>

The painting caused a lot of controversy because it shows a pipe and at the same time claims, «this is not a pipe». Of course, Magritte is right &mdash; what he painted is _an image of a pipe_. He delivered the proof as a question: «<i>Can I smoke it?</i>»

So, looking at the Semantic Eye logo with an actual «semantic eye» (see above) let's you see it for what it is: _a framed picture of a hand whose index finger points to the right_.

Such pictures of hands are called _manicules_, and they have been understood and used for almost a thousand years as _pointing to something noteworthy_ [[6]](/pages/references/index.html#6), [[7]](/pages/references/index.html#7). Thus, the Semantic Eye logo points practitioners to the _Semantic Eye Framework_, which provides them with effective tools and teaches them to look at complex problems from a different angle, allowing them to get a deeper understanding and to create a _meanignful and correct big picture_ (among other things).
