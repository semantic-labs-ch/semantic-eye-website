<div class="single-column-text top-separator">
    <h1>Why do Transformations get into Trouble?</h1>
    <p>Why is it so hard for organisations to create their products and provide their services with digital support and automation? After all, other industries have been using digital tools with great success for a long time (e.g. Construction, Manufacturing, Logistics, Medicine).</p>
    <h2>Top 3 Reasons</h2>
    <p>Here are the top 3 reasons why organisation are struggling or failing in their digital transformations:</p>
</div>

<div class="card-container">
    <div class="image-text-card">
        <img  src="/assets/card-icons/complexity.svg" alt="Icon for complexity">
        <div class="card-text-content">
            <h3>Massive Complexity</h3>
            <p>A digital transformation is an <em>exercise in massive complexity</em> over many dimensions.</p>
            <p>It is very «easy» to <em>overlook or misjudge</em> important facts and dependencies. Often with <em>disastrous consequences</em>.</p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="image-text-card">
        <img  src="/assets/card-icons/difficult.svg" alt="Icon for difficulty">
        <div class="card-text-content">
            <h3>Automation and Innovation</h3>
            <p><em>Digitalisation is automation</em>, and automation is <em>hard</em> in any industry.</p>
            <p>Digitalisation is also innovation, and innovation requires a <em>rare combination of skills</em>: knowledge, creativity and pragmatism.</p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="image-text-card">
        <img  src="/assets/card-icons/hard.svg" alt="Icon for hard work">
        <div class="card-text-content">
            <h3>Lack of Methodology and Tools</h3>
            <p>Digital transformation is a young industry; current <em>methodology is amateurish</em><a href="/pages/references/index.html#5">[5]</a>.</p>
            <p><em>No truly digital tools</em> are being used before actual software development starts.</p>
            <p>(See section further down)</p>
        </div>
    </div>
</div>

<div class="single-column-text">
    <p class="take-away">Today, organisations have no grip on these monumental challenges.</p>
</div>

<!-- Looking for Help -->
<div class="single-column-text">
    <h2>Looking for Help</h2>
    <p>Being aware of the risks and problems of a digital transformation, organisations seek help from IT partners. This often creates an unholy alliance where <em>«the blind is leading the blind»</em>.</br>Invariably, it leads to domain specialists becoming forever <em>dependent</em> on specific software people &mdash; who usually move on after a few years.</p>
</div>

<!-- Lack of Methodology and Tools -->
<div class="single-column-text">
    <h2>Lack of Methodology and Tools</h2>
    <p>Let's illustrate what this actually means in relation to e.g. Building Contstruction. While building architects have been using such wonderful tools as <em>CAD</em> or <em>BIM</em> since the 1970s, professionals who digitalise organisational processes continue to use the <em>digital equivalents of pencils and paper</em>.</p>
</div>

<div class="card-container">
    <div class="image-text-card">
        <img  src="/assets/card-icons/building-construction.svg" alt="Icon">
        <div class="card-text-content">
            <h3>Building Construction</h3>
            <p>The future building owner talks to an architect. The architect knows how buildings and builders work. She draws true-to-scale blueprints and hands them to the builders.</p>
            <p><em>The blueprints act as a precise, shared language</em>.</p>
            <p>The builders execute what the blueprints describe.</p>
        </div>
    </div>
    <div class="image-text-card">
        <img  src="/assets/card-icons/software-construction.svg" alt="Icon">
        <div class="card-text-content">
            <h3>Software Construction</h3>
            <p>Especially in projects with agile methodologies, the future product owner («building owner») talks directly to the software developer («excavator guy»).</p>
            <p><em>There is no shared language.<br>There are no solution blueprints.</em></p>
            <p>«<i>A bit more to the right, deeper ... deeper &mdash; that's it!</i>»</p>
        </div>
    </div>
</div>

<div class="single-column-text">
    <p class="take-away">A construction blueprint is a <em>conceptual, shared model of the future building</em>.<br>
    It is <em>a binding contract (specification)</em> for all involved parties.</p> 
</div>
